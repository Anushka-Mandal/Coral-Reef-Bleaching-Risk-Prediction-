Why it matters

The reef is running out of time to recover

When the sea stays too warm for too long, corals expel the algae that feed and colour them. They turn white and, if the heat lasts, they starve. Early warning gives reef managers time to act.

Heat stress builds up

Bleaching follows accumulated heat, not a single hot day. NOAA tracks this as Degree Heating Weeks: above 4, significant bleaching is likely; above 8, severe bleaching and coral death are likely.

It keeps happening

Mass bleaching has struck the Great Barrier Reef again and again, with less time to recover between events.

20162017202020222024

Why the north & centre

The northern and central reef took the heaviest damage in the back-to-back 2016 and 2017 events. Much of it is remote, so satellites are often the first to see trouble.

What we predict

Five levels of bleaching risk

We use NOAA Coral Reef Watch's Bleaching Alert Area scale, the standard used by reef managers worldwide. Our model forecasts the highest level each reef area will reach over the coming week, up to 14 days ahead.

How it works

From satellite pixels to plain-language risk

  1. 01

    Satellite ocean data

    Daily 5 km maps from NOAA Coral Reef Watch: sea surface temperature, temperature anomaly, HotSpot and Degree Heating Weeks.

    Built
  2. 02

    Deep learning forecast

    A CNN-ConvLSTM reads four weeks of ocean data plus the weather forecast, predicts HotSpot for the next 14 days, and a built-in NOAA formula turns that into DHW and NOAA's 7-day maximum alert for every reef cell.

    Built
  3. 03

    Reef report knowledge

    AIMS, Marine Monitoring Program and Reef Authority reports are split into 3,180 searchable passages; the most relevant ones published before the chosen date are retrieved for each briefing.

    Built
  4. 04

    LLM explanation

    A large language model combines the forecast, today's numbers and the retrieved report passages into a cited, plain-language explanation.

Roadmap

Where the project is heading

  1. Satellite data pipelineNOAA downloads, cropping and 4-channel daily stacks, 2018–2025.
  2. Forecast modelsCNN-LSTM, CNN-GRU and ConvLSTM compared, with dropout and early-stopping variants.
  3. Report embeddingsAIMS, MMP and Reef Snapshot reports indexed with BGE-large; fusion with the CNN evaluated.
  4. Live reef mapReal NOAA data clipped to the Marine Park, with reef-level statistics.
  5. Corrected forecast pipelineRaw NOAA values, calendar-aware sequences, season-based train / validation / test split, and baselines to beat.
  6. Forecast on the mapThe chosen model's 7-day-ahead alert level for every reef cell, run on live NOAA data.
  7. LLM explanationsStreamed, cited briefings and Q&A grounded in the live numbers, the forecast and the reef reports.
  8. Physics-guided 14-day forecastNOAA's DHW formula built into the network, weather forecasts as input, tested with real archived forecasts.
  9. More reef regionsExtend to the southern GBR and other reef systems, and compare with NOAA's operational outlook.
